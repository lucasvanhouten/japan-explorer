# A worked example — 12 nights in April, food and temples

*One finished run of the kit, so you can see what you are aiming at. Nothing here was invented for
the example: every place to stay is on the kit's own accommodation shortlist, every band is the one
that table prints, and every travel time and mode is a row of the kit's corridor and airport tables.
The blocks below are the kit's `Trip profile` and its itinerary table, filled in.*

**Who asked:** a couple in their thirties, first trip to Japan, twelve nights in April, "we're here
for the food and the temples", comfortable budget with room for one special night.

---

## Stage 1 — the interview

**Trip profile**

- **Dates** — mid-April 2027 · 12 nights
- **Flying into** — not booked yet; Tokyo assumed, and the reverse was priced in Stage 5
- **Travellers** — a couple, no children
- **Japan before** — first trip
- **Draws** — food and drink · temples, gardens and history
- **Taste** — long lunches, markets and walking; they book two or three meals and leave the rest to chance, and "we did nothing on Thursday" was said as praise
- **Pace** — settle in
- **Budget** — comfortable, with one splurge night
- **Fixed** — nothing booked yet
- **Wish list** — one proper traditional inn, a market, no theme parks
- **Assumed** — pace (they said "not on trains all the time" — read as settle-in, so three bases rather than six); mid-April rather than late April

Mid-April was chosen for them and named: the blossom is usually finished in Kyoto by then and the
crowds have thinned, and it lands clear of Golden Week, the national holiday week at the end of April
when the whole country travels at once.

## Stage 2 — what they picked from the cards

Shown first: Tokyo, Kyoto, Nara and Osaka, the food-and-temples cards. Offered in a single line and
declined: Kanazawa, Hakone, Hiroshima and the Inland Sea. They liked the sound of Nara — the oldest
temples, deer in the park — which turned it from a half-day trip into a stop of its own.

| Place | Known for | Nights |
|---|---|---|
| Tokyo | "the food, obviously" — markets, neighbourhoods, and a soft landing | 4–7 |
| Kyoto | the temples and gardens they had in mind when they said Japan | 4–6 |
| Nara | the oldest temples, and the deer | 1–2 |
| Osaka | eating, and cheaper rooms if Kyoto is full | 2–3 |

## Stage 3 — where to go

Shape 1, the classic first trip, bent once: Nara gets its own nights instead of being a day trip out
of Kyoto, and the hot-spring stop between the cities is dropped. That buys the traditional-inn night
they asked for without adding a fourth base. Osaka came off the longlist — a fourth base in twelve
nights, and Kyoto had rooms.

## Stage 4 — where to stay

One thing was said out loud, and it belongs in any run of this kit: **the built-in hotel shortlist
covers design and luxury properties only.** At a comfortable budget even its cheapest entries
are a stretch, and Japan is full of good mid-priced city hotels the kit does not carry. So the two
hotels below are the shortlist's most affordable entries, named as such, with a plain warning that
an ordinary booking site will show cheaper rooms in both cities. The inn is different — the
shortlist covers that tier well, and it is where the splurge goes.

**Tokyo**

| Stay | Price | Why |
|---|---|---|

**Nara**

| Tsukihitei · inn · A-tier · 8.6 / 10 · [write-up](https://ryokancatalog.com/inn/tsukihitei) · [book on Ikyu](https://www.ikyu.com/00001181/) | $750–950, dinner and breakfast for two | A five-room 1902 hideaway inside Nara's primeval forest, renovated 2023–25, and the reason the trip ends in Nara. No bath in the rooms. |
| Fufu Nara · inn · A-tier · 8.5 / 10 · [write-up](https://ryokancatalog.com/inn/fufu-nara) · [book on Ikyu](https://www.ikyu.com/00002739/) | $780–1200, dinner and breakfast for two | An open-air hot-spring bath on the room's own terrace, and beds rather than floor mattresses — the answer if communal bathing or a bad back is a worry. |

Both inns were introduced with one line about where they are booked: ikyu.com is a Japanese booking
site with an English front, and it is the easiest place to book most ryokan — the inn's own page or
an email where Ikyu has no listing.

The splurge goes on the inn, and it gets two nights rather than one: it is the only inn stay of the
trip, and they asked for a settle-in pace. Every other stop would have been one night at an inn.

Two follow-up questions were asked, because only two changed the pick: whether either
of them had tattoos or would rather not bathe communally (the Fufu Nara earmark is the answer to
that), and whether a long formal Japanese dinner sounded like a highlight or a chore.

Those answers fill the `Stay options` column the table left empty: the lean marked *(chosen)*, the second name kept in the running.

**Totals:** 6h00 total transit · 3 separate stays · 2 inn nights / 10 city nights · 0
one-nighters — `36min + 2h45 + 45min + 1h54 (out to the airport) = 6h00`.

**Notes:** land in the afternoon, so day one in Tokyo is a shower, a walk and dinner nearby · forward
the big suitcase from the Tokyo hotel on the morning you leave for Kyoto · Tsukihitei's check-in is
mid-afternoon and dinner is at a set hour, so no excursion that day. **Assumed:** settle-in pace;
mid-April dates; the splurge spent on the inn rather than the hotels.

**To confirm**

1. The arrival transfer, once the flight is booked — the 35 minutes above is Haneda, and Narita is a
   different and longer journey.
2. Tsukihitei's dinner sitting, and whether it takes a dietary request.
3. Both hotels' cancellation terms, before booking anything else.

Both orderings were priced.

| Order | Total transit | Separate stays | Inn / city nights | One-nighters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tokyo → Nara | 6h00 | 3 | 2 / 10 | 0 |
| Nara → Tokyo | 6h00 | 3 | 2 / 10 | 0 |

Reversed, the internal travel is identical, but the inn night then lands on day two, when jet lag is
at its worst and a ten-course dinner at seven in the evening is wasted. The order above wins on jet
lag rather than travel time.

Two things the plan deliberately does *not* have. No fourth stop: a fifth night in Tokyo is worth
more to a first-timer than a first night in a sixth town. And only one inn stay, so the long formal
dinners never stack.
